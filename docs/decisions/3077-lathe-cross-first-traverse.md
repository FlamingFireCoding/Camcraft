## #3077 - The first traverse of a cross drill on a lathe or a mill-turn

- **What was decided** (issue workers, unattended; the maintainer can change it):
  1. **The premise is the maintainer's (#2987, #2997).** The unknown start is the top of the machine's travel: on a lathe the turret's index position (the tool-change position), at the top of the X and Z travel. This is the same premise as the upright law and the 3+2 table law. NOT covered: a program that starts with the tool low near the work, for example after a manual jog.
  2. **The lathe post's real first move is judged.** `fanuc-lathe.cps` `liveMove` writes `G00 C` with the turret at the index position, then `G00 X Z` to the first motion record. The record stream (`camcraft_post::post::dump`) writes each move's END only; the first move's start goes out only as the `StartX/Y/Z` parameters, and `liveMove` does not move there. So the first G00 X Z goes to the END of move 0: the R-plane point of the first hole, not the kernel's standoff (`crates/motion/src/drilling/cross.rs`: the path starts at the standoff, move 0 is the rapid to the R plane). `tormach-lathe.cps` states no `liveTooling` and refuses a live section. A G00 moves each axis at its own rate (RS-274 / ISO 6983), so the tip can pass any point of the box between the index position and the first point: every radius from the first point's radius out, at every height from its Z up.
  3. **The law** (`camcraft_sim::verify::live_first_traverse`). The tool lies along the station's radius, its tip inward. The swept solid is read in the part frame about the spindle line through the work offset (`u` the first point's radial direction, `v` across it, `s = u - r`): `u >= r`, `|v| <= W(s)`, `z >= z - W(s)`. `W(s)` is the widest band of the tool stack (`ToolGeom`: flute, shank, holder) within `s` of the tip. Past the stack's end the widest band runs on, because the turret is not modelled. The move's end is judged, then its start (on one station line the start's swept solid lies inside the end's).
     - Work and turning steel stand at the C angle the post states before X and Z move, so they are read exactly: each facet (a box as its twelve facets) is clipped by each band's planes. Work: the source (the part, or the stock on a stock source), the kept parts, the neighbour parts, and the setup's stock (its facets, or its box when it has no facets). Turning steel: the chuck (`fixture::workholding_for` `.0`).
     - Fixed steel (a tailstock, the machine table) stands in the machine frame at an angle the part frame does not state. It is read revolved about the spindle: its nearest and farthest radius against each band, and its top against the band's floor. That holds at any C.
     - **A touch passes.** A face within `Z_TOL` (1e-6 mm) of the swept solid's boundary is clear, as in the other traverse laws: the tip slides down a face flush with the R plane and does not enter it. The work's model excess (a coarse model, #842) grows the swept solid by that excess, so on a coarse model the touch is a hit unless the kernel moved its R plane out by the excess (it does).
  4. **A refusal by name, not a warning.** The cross branch of generation refuses the drill (`GenError::Invalid`): "cross drill: the first rapid comes from the turret's index position ...". The refusal names the solid and how far out from the spindle it reaches, at which Z. No toolpath, so nothing posts. A start or a tool stack that cannot be read is refused by name too.
  5. **A mill-turn answers to two laws.** Its default post is the mill post (`posting::default_post_for`: `Kind::MillTurn` gives the builtin Fanuc mill post). That post sends Z to the clearance height, then X/Y from an unknown X/Y, then the full block with the rotary words (decision #2965 item 1). So a mill-turn cross drill also answers to the upright law (`first_traverse::judge` with the cross drill's lifted start). The live-tool law also judges it. A mill-turn that posts through a lathe post is judged by both laws, which is stricter than its own post.
  6. **Cache.** The cross drill's op stamp takes one more word, `live-traverse-3077`. Such a cache reads Stale once and the post refuses it until a regenerate.
- **Why.** The lathe post's move order is the fact that the law must follow. C is in position before X and Z move, so the work and the chuck have a known angle; the fixed steel has none in the part frame, so it is read at every angle. The tool's own stack, not its widest band everywhere, is what the rapid carries past the work: a wide shank 30 mm behind the tip cannot meet a lump 3 mm beside the drill's line.
- **Pinning tests.** `crates/app/tests/lathe_cross_drill.rs`: `a_cross_drill_over_its_chuck_has_a_clear_first_traverse` (lathe and mill-turn: clear), `a_cross_drill_under_a_wide_tailstock_is_refused_by_name` (lathe and mill-turn: refused), `a_thin_tailstock_centre_is_clear_on_a_lathe_and_convicts_a_mill_turn`, `a_turning_collar_flush_with_the_first_point_is_clear_and_one_proud_of_it_is_refused`. The sim unit tests in `crates/sim/src/verify/live_traverse_tests.rs` pin the tool width, the touch, the coarse excess, the shank band behind the flute, the station's half-plane, a square bar's face and diagonal, the clipped facet, the spindle line off the origin, the fixed steel read revolved, and a start or a tool that cannot be read. The shipped cross-drill suites stay green unchanged (`coarse_cross_drill`, `drilling_cross`, `drilling_cross_box` and the others).
- **Known limits.**
  - The law does not use the stated X and Z travel. It reads every height from the band's floor up, and every radius out. Steel beyond the travel top is read as in the way. That is stricter.
  - The turret itself (its body and the other stations) is not modelled. Past the tool stack the widest band of the stack runs on; a turret wider than that is not read.
  - The tool's cross-section is read as a square of its band's half-width about its axis, not a disc. That is stricter by the corners.
  - A lathe whose index position is at the low Z end (toward the chuck) is not this premise. The shipped machines and the lathe convention of this project put the free end of the bar at +Z.
  - The other kinds on a mill-turn (milling, axial drilling) still answer to no first-traverse law (`is_mill` is false). That is outside #3077: #3083 owns it.
- **What was considered and dropped**:
  - A new `Warning` variant: it needs mappings in the UI, MCP and the warned moves, and `FirstTraverseLow` says "unknown X/Y", which a lathe does not have. A refusal by name in the cross branch needs none.
  - The work and the chuck read revolved too (the first draft): it refused every legal cross drill on a square bar or a box stock (the corner radius stands over the face's station), 22 tests in the cross-drill suites. The swept solid reads them at the angle the post states.
  - One slab as wide as the widest band (the second draft): it refused a wide shank that stands 30 mm behind the tip (`drilling_cross_box::a_wide_shank_on_box_stock_is_proven_clear_along_the_line`).
  - A touch read as a hit (the second draft took the tolerance off the radius): it refused a flange flush with the R plane at full fidelity (`coarse_cross_drill`), which no other traverse law refuses.
  - Judging only the kernel's standoff (move 0's start): the lathe post never moves there.
  - Vertex samples in the station's half-plane: a large facet can cross the band with no vertex in it. Each facet is clipped instead.
  - Judging only the mill law on a mill-turn: a mill-turn can post through a lathe post, and the last block goes down at the station's radius.
- **Cost to change later**: low. One sim law (`live_traverse.rs`), one app seam (`operation::cross_traverse`), one call in the cross branch and one flag at the upright judge.
