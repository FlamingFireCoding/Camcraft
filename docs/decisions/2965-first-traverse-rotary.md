## #2965 - The first traverse of the rotary, multi-axis and cross-drill kinds

- **What was decided** (issue worker, unattended; the maintainer can change it):
  1. **The post order is the basis.** After a tool change the post sends Z, then X/Y at that height, then the full block with the rotary words (`fanuc.cps` `xyFirst` and `onRapid`). So the X/Y traverse runs before the rotaries move to the pose of the operation. The rotaries stand at a pose that the operation does not state. The Z of that traverse is the higher of the operation's clearance height and the start (`traverseZ`). The #2965 kinds are read at that height: a rotary or multi-axis path opens at its own pose (for example a radial clearance beside a standing bar, at Z -10), and the post does not traverse there. The #2901 kinds keep their reading of the start (their start is on the clearance plane, and the start is never higher than the traverse, so that reading is the stricter one).
  2. **Rotary kinds** (`Mode::Rotary`: Rotary Contour, Rotary Parallel, Rotary Offset). A new law, `sim::verify::first_traverse_low_swept`, reads the work as swept through a full turn about its fitted axis:
     - The start stock's and the part's points are read at their swept top (`swept_top`: the foot on the axis plus the radius, scaled by `sqrt(1 - dz^2)`). A 40 x 40 bar on a horizontal axis reads 28.284 over the axis, not its upright top of 20.
     - The steel that turns with the part (`Workholding::turning`, a chuck) is read swept too, at its corners (the distance to a line is convex, so the maximum is at a corner). The steel that stands still (`Workholding::fixed`, the table, a tailstock) is read at its top in the machine frame.
     - The start's machine height is `origin.z + t . (start - origin)`, with `t` the tool axis of move 0 (`+Z` when the move states none; the frame's axis for a 3+2 path). The machine stands the tool upright by a turn about the axis line. That turn keeps the line and maps `t` onto `+Z`. A radial start reads its radius over the axis line. When no turn about the axis stands the tool upright (a live tool beside a C axis, `stamp_spindle_c`), the start is read at the lowest height that the turn can carry it to: its own Z for a vertical C axis.
     - Which law reads a rotary kind depends on the mounted machine (`AxisLimits::turns_part` of its tilting rotaries). Each one turns the part: the swept law only (the post writes machine heights). Each one rides the head: the tip law of item 3 only (the work never turns, and an RTCP post writes the tip in the part frame). No machine, a mixed machine, or a rotary that an old file never stated: both laws, and the first verdict is kept.
     - The verdict is the same `Warning::FirstTraverseLow` (blocking, MCP `first_traverse_low`), so the post gate, the UI, MCP and the warned moves need no change.
     - The axis is the job's (`fixture::rotary_axis_of`). When the job states none (a stock or selection source), the same fit of the same facets that the generator ran is used. If no axis can be fitted, generation is refused by name ("no rotary axis can be fitted to the part, so its first traverse cannot be judged"). The generator itself fits that axis, so this refusal is not expected in use.
  3. **Multi-axis kinds** (5-Axis Contour, 5-Axis Parallel, Swarf, Advanced Swarf, Multi-Axis Rough, 5-Axis Flow, Multi-Axis Contour). They answer to the #2901 law on their tip, upright in the part frame (or along the plane's axis in a 3+2 frame). Reason: the post writes the tip in the part frame, so the Z move and the X/Y traverse run on the part's own plane at the start height. The tool leans up from its tip in each pose that cuts from above, so the stock and the fixtures under that plane are under the whole tool.
  4. **Cross drill on a mill** (`OpParams::Drill` with a `cross` list). Generation refuses it by name before any move ("cross holes are live-tooling work - the mounted machine is a mill with no turret"), so it can have no first traverse. The test pins that refusal. The selection still includes the kind, so the tip law reads it if a mill ever generates radial holes.
  5. **Cross drill on a lathe or a mill-turn** (`Kind::Lathe`, `Kind::MillTurn`, live tooling) is not judged by this law. `is_mill` is false there, as for the turning kinds of #2901. A lathe turret does not traverse in a mill's X/Y plane. Its radial approach is judged by the cross-drill standoff (`cross_stock::standoff`, the raw stock radius) and the rapid laws. This decision records that limit; it is not proof that the lathe approach has no collision. #3077 owns that gap.
- **Cache.** The post gate trusts the verdicts that a cache carries. The op stamp of the multi-axis kinds, the rotary kinds and the cross drill (`first_traverse::newly_judged`) takes one more word, `first-traverse-2965`. Such a cache reads Stale once and the post refuses it until a regenerate. The salt `camcraft-op-stamp-v4` does not change, so no other kind's stamp moves and the pinned stamps (`deburr_cache_stamp`, `wire_stamp`) stay. Dropped: a salt v5 for all kinds (each saved cache and each pinned stamp moves, for kinds whose law did not change).
- **Example projects (the maintainer accepted: regenerate, stamp only).** The new stamp word makes two committed projects read Stale once. They are regenerated, as #2901 regenerated its four examples. The diff is the operation stamp fields only, and each `.nc` and golden stays byte-identical:
  - `examples/first-continuous-4axis-part.camproj` (operation `Journal`, a continuous 4-axis kind): by its own test, `WRITE_GOLDEN=1 cargo test -p camcraft-app --test continuous_4axis_part`.
  - `examples/guide-multiaxis.camproj` (operation `Swarf1`): loaded, `Swarf1` regenerated with `GenerateOperation`, saved with `project::save` (the #2901 path). Its own path, `cargo xtask docs-images`, builds all three guide projects again and renders the guide images, which changes more than the stamp; it was not used. `Indexed Face1` (a face, not a #2965 kind) keeps its stamp.
- **Pinning tests.** `crates/app/tests/first_traverse_rotary.rs`: `a_rotary_kind_reads_its_square_bar_swept_about_the_axis`, `a_5_axis_kind_reads_a_far_clamp_over_its_clearance_plane`, `a_cross_drill_on_a_mill_is_refused_before_any_traverse`. The sim unit tests in `crates/sim/src/verify/first_traverse.rs` pin the swept law (corner radius, radial start, chuck and tailstock, axis tilt, a C axis, the clearance height).
- **Why one law per frame and no new verdict.** The #2901 verdict already blocks the post, carries its two heights and has its UI, MCP and warned-move mappings. A new variant would add four mappings and no new information. The words "clearance height Z..." read the machine height over the axis for a rotary kind, which is the clearance radius when the axis is at Z 0.
- **Known limits.**
  - A machine with two rotaries: the rotary kinds read the sweep about the one fitted axis only. The multi-axis kinds read the tip in the part frame; a pose that leans the tool down from its tip (an undercut) is not read.
  - The tool radius is not added: the law reads the tip, as the #2901 law does. A horizontal tool (cross drill) can reach one radius under its tip line.
- **What was considered and dropped**:
  - The rotary kinds read at the start angle only (no sweep): wrong, because the rotary words come after the X/Y traverse.
  - A sweep for the multi-axis kinds about each machine rotary: the machine's axis lines are not stated in the part frame for those kinds (`rotary_axis_of` is `None` for them), so the sweep has no line to turn about.
  - A refusal of every multi-axis and cross-drill operation (no law, only a refusal): it blocks programs that the tip law shows to be safe.
  - The judge in `camcraft-motion`: the verdict and the steel types are in `camcraft-sim`, as for #2901. No crate edge is added.
- **Cost to change later**: low. One law function and the selection in `operation::first_traverse` (`answers`, `turned_axis`).
